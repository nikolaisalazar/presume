## Mission and two readers

| Field | Proposed resolution |
| --- | --- |
| Shared thesis | Resume tools should preserve direct control over the finished document. |
| Primary reader | A job seeker, initially comfortable with a desktop browser and a structured resume, who needs to update an existing career story. |
| Primary task | Make a meaningful edit, understand its layout consequences, download the intended PDF, and return to the same work safely. |
| Primary read | Edit directly on the resume page, choose formatting limits, inspect the result, and keep a usable copy. |
| Second reader | A design or engineering portfolio reviewer evaluating judgment, implementation depth, and validation. |
| Second read | See why the document, saving, export, and optional review have their particular boundaries, including the tradeoffs and evidence behind repairs. |
| Desired final impression | A precise, calm tool whose behavior earns the visual confidence it projects. |
| Primary action | Open the editor; use Continue editing when a valid saved document exists. |
| Secondary action | Inspect the edit-to-export example, then the project explanation and source. |

Job seekers remain the primary audience. Portfolio depth should reward a second
read without making a first-time user understand a library or architecture
before trying the product. The existing fixed US Letter output and desktop-first
workbench give the next release a useful boundary. The Review rubric's software
engineering focus belongs beside Review, rather than narrowing every editing task.

**Proposed product promise:** Presume lets you edit your resume directly on the
page, see the effect of your formatting choices, and carry the intended document
forward as a PDF and a restorable backup. You decide what the resume says.

That is the target promise for remediation, not a claim that the audited baseline
already fulfills it. Dependable saving, complete restore, readable output, and
layout agreement need the evidence identified below.

The complete basic task requires no account or review service. Retain the current
scope: one active local document, US Letter PDF, direct editing, explicit fit
constraints, and optional advisory review. Cloud synchronization, accounts,
template marketplaces, automatic writing, PDF/DOCX import, and a public hosted
review service are separate product commitments. Named copies, reordering, and
faster transfer of existing text are useful research candidates after recovery
works; they are not additions to the current implementation scope.

## Recommended evolution: show the document becoming useful

The core argument is a visible sequence: a person changes the wording, judges the
formatting response, and gets a usable document. Ownership is the interpretation
of that experience. It should have a shorter supporting role once the experience
is clear.

Use a hybrid sequence with a direct product introduction, concrete proof,
practical questions, and a deeper project explanation. A conventional feature
grid, testimonials, or separate process chapter would repeat this short argument
or require evidence we do not have. The live editor remains the working product;
the landing should not become a second resume editor.

The emotional impression remains calm and competent. Keep Geist application
text, the serif document, Mineral Quiet fields, restrained Verdigris, rectilinear
controls, and the green-black closing territory. Change narrative weight before
changing visual identity. The main tradeoff is less space for the ownership
manifesto and the Pretext instrument. Better product understanding is the
hypothesis to test, not an established conversion result.

The primary proof should use still captures initially. They keep the sequence
inspectable, work without motion, and avoid implementing a new interactive
simulation. Captions describe what actually changed; the source document and
downloaded PDF must be the same example. A later user-controlled recording can
supplement the stills if it clarifies causality. Do not invent fit readouts or
render a proposed UI as a capture of the working product.

The deeper project material can use an in-page overview linking to a repository
case study. It does not require a new application route. T5 should choose the
final placement of the existing Pretext instrument after a whole-page study;
its measurement behavior and truthful attribution remain useful.

### Representative copy for selecting the register

These samples establish plain, specific language. They are conditional on the
corresponding repairs and have not replaced any approved production copy.

**Hero**

> Edit your resume directly on the page.
>
> Set your page and line limits, edit your text, and download a PDF. Your work
> saves in this browser, with no account needed.
>
> Open the editor

Use **Continue editing** for a valid saved document. Prefer **No account needed**
beside a fresh-visit CTA to a statement implying that new work has already saved.
Keep **Your resume should stay yours** as a supporting ownership line if it earns
its place without repeating the surrounding copy. The save sentence requires
T2's truthful save-state behavior; it is not ready to publish on the baseline.

**Core evidence**

> See what changes as you edit.
>
> Add a longer bullet, adjust the line limit, and inspect the exported page.

Pair this with the real sequence. Captions should identify the edit and actual
constraint values recorded during capture, rather than adding illustrative
numbers now. Link text may read **Inspect the sample PDF** once that exact artifact
exists. If the chosen limits cannot be met, the example should show the warning
and the user's response instead of implying that every document fits automatically.

**Continuity**

> Pick up where you left off.
>
> This browser keeps your current resume. Download a backup to move it to another
> browser or keep an extra copy. Clearing browser data can remove the saved draft.

Use **Download backup** and **Restore backup**, with JSON described as the format
in supporting text. After F09 is fixed, explain that the backup includes resume
text and formatting settings. Do not promise cloud recovery or transfer through
the PDF. Recovery, migration, and import failure wording must follow T2's actual
outcomes.

**Review**

> Get another perspective on your resume.
>
> Optional Review returns observations and suggestions. You choose which ones
> to use. The current rubric is intended for software-engineering resumes.

If the existing specimen is retained, its representative advice remains
**Add one production metric**, with **Illustrative result; not calculated from
this resume.** visible beside it. That sentence deliberately proposes a reopening
of the locked fixture label. It does not turn the deterministic fixture into
content-derived evidence. Use no hiring, ATS, outcome, or score-improvement claim.

**Project**

> How Presume keeps the document under your control.
>
> Explore the decisions behind direct editing, recoverable local work, and
> separate advisory review.

The linked case study needs actual decisions, tradeoffs, and repair evidence.
Dependency names should answer how a decision was implemented; they should not
carry the whole project story.

## Desktop and narrow composition

**Desktop:** retain an asymmetric copy-and-document hero, with a visible primary
CTA and an authentic Letter page on the right. The next chapter carries the
largest evidence area: a legible editor detail showing the changed bullet and
relevant Fit state, followed by the actual PDF. Use a stable reading order across
the three captures. Short annotations belong outside the document artwork. The
continuity chapter becomes shorter and practical. Review keeps a pale field with
its observation and suggestion more prominent than the numeric score. The project
chapter has enough space for evidence and reasoning without another oversized
text-only interlude. Keep the centered terminal action.

**Narrow:** use a single content column, 16px gutters, and a full-width hero CTA.
Remove the viewport-height requirement that currently leaves room for text alone.
Place a legible authentic editor detail immediately after the hero action, before
the longer explanation. It should show a text field in its real document context;
label any crop as a detail rather than a full-page view. The evidence sequence
stacks edit, fit, and export in the same order, with captions available as text.
The full PDF remains proportional and separately inspectable. Never shrink a
complete desktop dashboard into an unreadable mobile picture or stretch the page
to fill a box.

At 320px, let copy wrap naturally and let advice/evidence stack if necessary.
Final type sizes, hero line count, crop bounds, and breakpoint positions need a
browser study. The three-line H1 cap and forced two-column narrow Review specimen
are candidates for reopening if they conflict with readable real copy. The task,
action, and first product evidence should form one continuous opening; this brief
does not claim they have been proven to fit any specific viewport.

Keep semantic headings, a skip link, visible keyboard focus, meaningful alt text,
44px important action targets, and readable disclosures. A media failure should
leave the caption and invitation usable. Reserve intrinsic dimensions for media;
load deeper captures only as needed. Motion may show causality or state, with a
static equivalent and user control. No automatic playback, pinned scroll,
parallax, or new ornamental motion is proposed. Existing landing motion should
be re-evaluated where the section carrying it is removed or recomposed.

The prior-approved preset, rather than the skill's upstream default, governs
this pass. It retains Geist, capped prose, restrained accents, flat backgrounds,
Phosphor icons, and purposeful motion. It does not adopt a fluid-island header,
word-reveal tagline, gradients, or a new spacing/radius system. Typography,
spacing, hero crop, section length, and Review hierarchy are the focused adjustment
categories for the next study. There are no accepted new visual overrides yet.

## First use, repeat use, and Review choices

T2/T3 should settle behavior before T5 writes state labels around it. The practical
first-use target is one successful edit and export, with enough orientation to
understand saving and formatting. Retain immediate access to the sample. A short,
dismissible **Click any text to edit** instruction can sit near the document;
keyboard users need a corresponding discoverable editing path and named fields.
This is a proposed orientation change, not a replacement of the direct-editing
interaction model.

Make Download PDF the primary file action. Group backup and restore by purpose;
place reset in a secondary action surface with a recovery path. Use consistent
sample naming. Show actual save outcomes, current printed size, actual pages, and
the reason a limit cannot be met when those facts are reliable. Do not present a
requested one-page limit as the actual exported page count. Narrow users should
receive a clear cue for the fixed canvas and be able to reach common quick edits;
this does not promise a mobile-first editor redesign.

The proposed repeat-use contract is one current document with understandable
recovery. Undo should cover destructive changes. Save failures and tab conflicts
must preserve work until the user can resolve or export it. The exact history
lifetime, transaction boundaries, and conflict actions belong to T2's plan.
Only describe those outcomes in copy after they are selected and implemented.

**Recommended public Review choice:** retain the static public editor without a
bundled review endpoint for this remediation milestone. Explain Review as an
optional capability of a separately configured installation. Keep setup mechanics
in the developer guide. In the public editor, proposed explanatory copy is:
**This public editor does not include a Review service.** Link to an example and
setup only when those destinations actually exist and serve that purpose.

A successful recorded local example would make the capability inspectable for a
portfolio reviewer without requiring a hosted service. It remains a future T4/T5
deliverable. Until then, retain a clearly illustrative specimen or omit claims
that require a real model result. Before configured submission, show where the
PDF will go, the model/provider, and whether enrichment is enabled. Keep cancel,
retry, stale-result, unavailable, and failure states understandable. The rubric
and score must remain advisory.

Public hosting would create ongoing cost, availability, processing-disclosure,
and abuse-control obligations. It should be selected as a separate product and
operations decision after T4, rather than introduced as a landing-page dependency.

## Decision ledger and handoff

**Keep:** direct control thesis; job seekers as the primary reader; immediate
editor access; Mineral Quiet and the document/UI type relationship; authentic
Letter output; optional advisory Review; truthful example labeling; dependency
attribution; saved-document CTA behavior; simple navigation and dark ending.

**Propose changing:** task-first hero; evidence before the manifesto; visible
narrow product evidence; shorter continuity copy; plain-language file actions;
advice before score; deeper Presume-specific reasoning and a direct project link.

**Reject for this direction:** fabricated customer proof or outcome metrics;
unsupported perfect-fit, privacy, or ATS claims; a second editor on the landing;
new visual identity, ornamental navigation, or automatic media; accounts, cloud
sync, and public model hosting as prerequisites to fixing the current product.

**Open for selection:** the direct-editing headline and copy register; the new
section order and narrow media reopening; keeping the public editor without a
review endpoint for this milestone. Recommended defaults are the choices in
this brief. No new design decision is marked user-approved. Media crops, final
copy, case-study length, printed readability targets, exact accessibility states,
and the real Review example require downstream evidence or design work.

T1 can be selected while T2 planning proceeds. T5 can prepare disposable studies

copy requiring a live example waits for T4. Dependency upgrades remain T4-owned,

Milestone 18 is complete when the user selects the direction and representative
register, the specific reopened decisions are recorded, and every material claim
has evidence or a named gate. This working document alone does not complete it.
The later implementation handoff must include complete copy, whole-page desktop

evidence used to verify it. T6 must record what real participants understood and
did; these are intended outcomes, not results already observed.
